## PROBLEM

![](./01.png)

Visually impaired students cannot take in graphic information visually, so they learn from the tactile graphics in braille textbooks or from separate tactile teaching aids. In today's classrooms, those materials are in severely short supply.

Tactile graphics in braille textbooks are hard to produce and to maintain, and they rarely cover every graphic in the original textbook, so students often get only a portion. Tactile aids are usually made by teachers themselves, a process that takes excessive time and money, and durability problems make them hard to keep in use.

These constraints seriously limit what visually impaired students have the chance to learn, and they put a heavy load on teachers. An efficient, accessible way to produce tactile materials is needed.

## APPROACH

To address this, Dot Inc. built Dot Canvas, an authoring tool that works with the company's own tactile display, Dot Pad. Dot Canvas is a web and mobile app platform designed so that non-specialists can create and share tactile graphics easily.

On the web platform anyone can create tactile graphics intuitively and store and share files through the cloud. The mobile app uses iPad touch and the Apple Pencil for a more direct making experience. It also supports VoiceOver and live drawing, so visually impaired users can create tactile graphics themselves.

With these features, Dot Canvas aims to lower the barrier to making tactile materials and to build a more sustainable and inclusive environment for tactile education.

## DOT CANVAS WEB

**An easy, capable web-based tool for making tactile graphics :** Dot Canvas Web is a web-optimized tool that lets non-specialists make tactile graphics without difficulty. A range of editing features and advanced options — PDF and image import among them — make more detailed content possible.

**Cloud storage and sharing for tactile graphics :** Dot Cloud stores tactile graphics and makes them available anywhere. The shared drive lets people exchange what they have made and keep a sustainable library of tactile material.

**Real-time graphic communication with multiple Dot Pads :** Up to 10 Dot Pads can connect and display the same content at once, so a group can experience a pre-made tactile graphic together in real time and talk about it.


## DOT CANVAS APP

**Making tactile graphics with touch and the Apple Pencil :** Dot Canvas App is a touch-based tactile-graphics tool optimized for iPad. Touch and the Apple Pencil make drawing more direct.

**Content management and sharing through the cloud :** Dot Cloud keeps tactile graphics stored and available at any time. Through the shared drive, several users can share content together and build up a body of tactile material.

**Accessibility features for visually impaired users :** It supports Apple's VoiceOver and live drawing, so visually impaired people can make tactile graphics themselves. Drawing and touching at once offers a new way to build tactile sense.

## IMPLEMENTATION

**Screen design and GUI in Figma :** Based on the user research, I did the screen design and GUI for Dot Canvas Web and App in Figma. I reworked the UI structure so users could find their way intuitively and placed each function clearly, arriving at an interface that both teachers and visually impaired students could use easily.

**User research through a mix of methods :** In Korea we ran field studies with special-education teachers and visually impaired students; for users abroad we used surveys and diary studies to look closely at the real usage environment and its pain points. The data became the main evidence for identifying problems in the first version of Canvas and setting the direction for improvements.

**Stronger developer collaboration and documentation :** Screen specs and functional specifications were documented systematically, and project issues were tracked in JIRA. That kept communication with the development team close, let technical changes be reflected quickly, and helped us meet the schedule for improvements and release.

**A better environment for visually impaired education :** Dot Canvas improved learning access for students and gave teachers an efficient tool for managing materials and supporting lessons.

**Revenue growth and investment :** Dot Canvas also played an important part in revenue growth and new funding. After it was introduced, demand in the visually impaired education market grew and the company passed 10 billion KRW in annual revenue in both 2023 and 2024. On the back of that growth, it raised about 15 billion KRW in additional investment.

**Global expansion and new collaborations :** Dot Canvas was shown at a range of international exhibitions, demonstrating what Dot Pad can do and its potential in the education market. It won the CES 2024 Innovation Award, which brought the technology to wider attention, and at international shows such as CSUN 2024 it drew collaboration proposals, widening the chances to work with educational institutions worldwide. All of this became a foothold for entering global markets.

## REFLECTION

**Weighing fast execution against long-term quality**

Dot Canvas was a demanding project: demo versions of both the web and the app had to ship in a short window. The team had little relevant experience at the time, and we had three months to produce something usable. We worked closely together, prototyped quickly, and took an execution-first approach. Along the way I built practical skills beyond UX design — collaborating with developers, managing schedules.

The limits of moving fast showed up just as clearly. We hit the short-term goal, but the design had not accounted for scale, so maintenance later required a lot of rework. The product was also rough in places, and parts of the experience were not satisfying. That taught me how much the balance between speed and quality matters in UX design.

Even so, what stays with me most is that it delivered value to real users. Hearing feedback directly in user studies taught me that UX design has to keep evolving. From here I want to approach UX design with more care for long-term scalability and maintenance, not just short-term delivery.
